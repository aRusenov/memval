## X.1 What "recall" means in MemVal

Every MemVal evaluation reduces to one operation: the model is given a partial
cue and must produce the remainder of the episode **without further input**.
Formally, for an episode `s = (s_1, …, s_T)` and a cue `s_{1:k}`, the model
returns `ŝ_{k+1:T}`; scoring compares `ŝ` to `s` position by position. This is
the whole contract, and it is deliberately stated at the level of the *task*
rather than the *mechanism*: MemVal's model interface exposes a single
`recall(cue, length)` call, and each model is free to satisfy it however its
architecture dictates.

We call this **autoregressive (generative) probing**, and contrast it with
**associative (cued) probing**, in which every position is probed independently
with the ground-truth predecessor supplied as the cue. The distinction is not
which model is used but whether ground truth is re-injected during recall.

The distinction matters for how the protocol should be read. Autoregressive
probing is often identified with *associative chaining* — the hypothesis that
serial order is carried by item-to-item associations — which a long line of work
on human serial recall has argued is insufficient on its own, favouring
positional or context-based codes instead [Lewandowsky & Murdock 1989; Henson
1998; Burgess & Hitch 1999]. MemVal takes no position in that debate. A model
that indexes items by position, or that retrieves an entire episode as a single
composite pattern and reads it out, satisfies the contract exactly as well as a
chaining model; our reference Equilibrium Propagation implementation happens to
satisfy it by iterated one-step prediction, but that is a property of the model,
not of the benchmark. What the protocol forbids is not positional coding — it is
the injection of ground-truth items during recall.

## X.2 Why recall is probed generatively

The case for withholding ground truth is that **teacher forcing has no correlate
in the retrieval regime of the circuit we are modelling.**

The hippocampus is characterized less by static storage than by its capacity to
generate ordered trajectories internally. Within the theta cycle, place-cell
assemblies sweep forward from the animal's current location to positions it has
not yet reached [O'Keefe & Recce 1993; Skaggs et al. 1996; Foster & Wilson
2007]. During sharp-wave ripples, replay traverses extended paths in either
direction while the animal is at rest [Wilson & McNaughton 1994; Foster & Wilson
2006; Davidson, Kloosterman & Wilson 2009], and a brief cue can launch a
sequence that runs ahead to a remote goal [Pfeiffer & Foster 2013]. In all of
these, the sequence is produced while the circuit is decoupled from the sensory
stream: the state at each step is driven by recurrent input — canonically the
CA3 collaterals, the standard substrate for auto- and hetero-associative
sequence storage [Marr 1971; McNaughton & Morris 1987; Treves & Rolls
1992/1994; Levy 1996; Lisman 1999] — and not by an external signal specifying
what should come next.

Clamping the correct item at each step therefore models the *encoding* regime,
in which the trajectory is sensory-driven, not the *retrieval* regime it is
meant to score. Our two protocols map onto that division directly: associative
probing measures what the sensory-clamped circuit has stored; autoregressive
probing measures what the decoupled circuit can regenerate. We report both, and
treat neither as redundant.

Two further properties follow from the same commitment.

**Partial-cue completion is the defining assay, not an add-on.** The canonical
CA3 manipulation — NMDA-receptor knockout restricted to CA3 — spares recall from
a full cue and impairs one-trial recall from a *partial* one [Nakazawa et al.
2002]. Sweeping the cue fraction `k/T` and rolling out the remainder is the
direct analogue, and it is available in MemVal as the `prompt_fraction`
dimension.

**Error cascade is a signature to reproduce, not an artifact to suppress.** In
human serial recall an error at one position degrades the positions after it,
and the resulting transposition gradients are among the strongest constraints on
models of order memory. A protocol that restores ground truth after every step
removes this phenomenon by construction, and with it the ability to ask whether
a model recovers from its own mistakes.

## X.3 Protocol validation: the two probes dissociate

The argument above motivates the protocol; the following establishes that it is
also *necessary*, in the strict sense that single-step probing cannot recover
the same ordering over models.

Table 1 reports, for the same trained model at the same list length, mean recall
rate (MRR) under associative probing and memory span under autoregressive
probing with raw feedback. Span is the longest prefix recalled above 0.75.

The two columns are close to uninformative about each other. At `L=7`, six of
the seven models sit at MRR ≥ 0.86 — a range of 0.14, near the ceiling of the
measure — while their spans range from 1 to 5. Asymmetric Hopfield and
EWC + DG-XdG EP are both at MRR = 1.00 at `L=5` and `L=7`, i.e. exactly tied
under cued probing, yet one sustains four to five items of autonomous recall and
the other cannot pass the first. The most extreme case is EWC + DG-XdG EP at
`L=11`: it answers 98% of single-step probes correctly and free-runs for one
item. Non-monotonicity is likewise invisible to the cued probe — Asymmetric
Hopfield's span rises from 4 to 5 and then collapses to 2 across the sweep while
its MRR stays pinned at 1.00.

The interpretation is that the two probes measure different things, exactly as
intended: cued MRR reports whether the transitions were stored, and span reports
whether the dynamics can be iterated. A model can have the first without the
second, and in our suite most do. Reporting only the associative probe would
have described six of these seven models as near-perfect sequence memories.

## X.4 Declared protocol parameters

Generative probing has one degree of freedom that single-step probing does not:
how the model's output is projected back onto the input manifold before the next
step. MemVal implements three modes and we report the choice explicitly, because
it changes the result by up to an order of magnitude.

- **`raw`** — the prediction is fed back verbatim. Magnitude is unconstrained,
  so the state can drift off the encoder's unit-norm manifold.
- **`l2`** — the prediction is renormalized to the unit sphere, matching the
  encoder's embeddings. Removes magnitude drift, retains direction error.
- **`quantized`** — the prediction is decoded to a symbol and that symbol's
  clean embedding is fed back. Drift-free, and only available where the domain
  is discrete.

Quantized feedback is not a neutral convenience. Four of the six models score a
perfect 10 under it while their raw spans are 1 or 2 — the mode restores the
state to a clean codebook entry at every step, so sub-threshold representational
degradation is erased before it can accumulate, and the measure saturates and
stops discriminating. It also silently advantages models whose outputs are
already symbol-like. We therefore report **`raw` as the headline mode**, `l2`
as the drift-corrected comparison that separates magnitude error from direction
error, and `quantized` only as a stated upper bound on what the stored
transitions could support if the state were externally cleaned up at every step.

**Exposure.** Arms are trained on a fixed per-arm budget in every section where
exposure is a control (the arm is brought to "good enough" and something else is
scored): hopfield 4, theta 4, dts_esn 1, temporal_pc 86, original_eqprop 220,
spiking_eqprop 22 (514 on spatial) epochs — each 2× the exposure at which the arm
reached criterion on the canonical 7-item list, the material every non-length
section uses. `sequence_length` sweeps L ∈ {3,…,11} within one category, so its
upper rungs may run under-trained for the slower arms; `criterion_reached` records
it, and that is the section's question. Where exposure is itself the result
(semantic similarity cost, schema acquisition, acquisition under load, interval
sections) the criterion ladder is kept. Budgets live in `MODEL_REGISTRY` and the
resolved source is written to each results file.

The remaining protocol parameters are fixed across the suite and reported with
each run: 100-dimensional embeddings with `category_variance = 0.2`, a **clean**
cue presented **once** per probe (`ProbeProtocol.CLEAN_SINGLE`; cue degradation
is swept only in `noise_invariance` and `cue_masking`, where it is the
manipulation — see `docs/sections/probe_protocol.md`), span threshold 0.75, and list
lengths `L ∈ {3, 5, 7, 9, 11}`. Results files that predate this carry no
`probe_protocols` entry and were produced with 30 draws of cue noise at
`σ = 0.05`; their sub-ceiling accuracies are basin fractions, not comparable
with clean numbers.

## X.5 Limitations

**Span is not decomposable.** Because a single early error zeroes the remainder,
span conflates per-step accuracy with dynamical stability, and it floors quickly
— at `L=11` most models in Table 1 sit at 1, where the measure discriminates no
better than the cued probe does at its ceiling. Span is therefore informative
over a middle band of lengths and should not be read outside it. The protocol
calls for two companion measures that recover the decomposition without
re-injecting ground truth: per-step accuracy under free-run conditioned on the
previous step being correct, and an error-cascade measure asking whether a model
that has stumbled returns to the correct trajectory. Both are specified in the
metric schedule; neither is emitted by the current symbolic pipeline, and the
results below report span alone. *(See TODOs — this must be resolved before the
section can claim them.)*

**Some models are being asked to do something they were not built to do.**
GPT-2's span is 0 at every length and every feedback mode: its free-running
accuracy at the first recalled position never clears the 0.75 threshold, so the
measure cannot resolve *how* it fails. This is a real cost of a common protocol.
We accept it because the alternative — scoring each model on the protocol its
architecture prefers — is precisely the incommensurability the benchmark exists
to remove.

**Alternatives not adopted.** Scheduled sampling [Bengio et al. 2015], which
interpolates between the two protocols at a mixing rate ε, would yield a
robustness curve rather than a point estimate; we do not use it because ε has no
referent in the biology and would introduce a second undeclared degree of
freedom alongside the feedback mode. Backward rollout is a natural extension
given that reverse replay is well attested [Foster & Wilson 2006], and we note
it as an additional axis rather than a substitute.
